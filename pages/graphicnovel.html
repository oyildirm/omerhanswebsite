Graphic Novel: Motherhood in Crisis Response

Reading “Motherhood in Crisis” honestly made me feel really uncomfortable, but in a way that I think was important. The stories in the graphic novel felt very real and personal, especially because they were based on actual experiences from women in Sierra Leone. A lot of the time when people hear statistics about poverty or childbirth deaths, it feels distant because it’s just numbers on a screen. But seeing these stories told through illustrations and personal experiences made everything feel much more human and emotional.

What affected me the most was how dangerous pregnancy can be for women in places without proper healthcare. Something that should normally be a safe and supported experience becomes life-threatening because of poverty, lack of hospitals, or social expectations. It was hard reading about women having almost no control over their own situations while still being expected to carry all the responsibility. Some of the stories felt hopeless at times, especially seeing how much suffering people had to go through just to survive everyday life.

I also think the graphic novel format made the stories more powerful. If this had just been a regular article with paragraphs of text, I probably would not have connected to it the same way. The artwork helped show emotions that words alone sometimes can’t explain. Certain panels felt quiet and heavy, especially the scenes showing fear, loneliness, or exhaustion. It made the experiences feel more personal instead of feeling like a distant news story happening somewhere else in the world.

Another thing I kept thinking about while reading was how unfair the situations were for many of the women. A lot of the problems they faced were caused by things completely outside of their control, like poverty, abuse, lack of education, or weak healthcare systems. It made me realize how different life can be depending on where someone is born. Things that many people take for granted, like access to doctors or safe hospitals, can completely change someone’s future.

I also liked that the stories didn’t just focus on sadness. Even though the situations were terrible, the women still showed strength and resilience. They kept trying to survive and protect their children despite everything happening around them. That part made the stories feel more real because it showed both suffering and perseverance at the same time.

Overall, I thought “Motherhood in Crisis” was really impactful because it made a global issue feel personal instead of abstract. It forced me to think more deeply about maternal healthcare, inequality, and the realities that many women face around the world. The combination of storytelling and artwork made the message much stronger, and I think it stayed with me more than a normal article would have. It’s the kind of story that makes you reflect on how many people struggle in ways that most of us never really see or think about.
